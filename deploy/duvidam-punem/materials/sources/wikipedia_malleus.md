## Encyclopedic Summary
The *Malleus Maleficarum* (first published in Speyer, Germany, in 1487) is the most famous and influential demonological treatise and legal-theological handbook on witchcraft of the early modern period. Designed to prove the existence of witchcraft, argue that women are inherently more susceptible to it, and provide a systematic procedural guide for identifying, prosecuting, and convicting witches, the book went through dozens of editions and circulated widely across Catholic and Protestant Europe.

---

## Structure and Content of the *Malleus*

The treatise is divided into three major Parts:

### Part I: The Reality and Theology of Witchcraft
- Focuses on proving that witchcraft is a real, physical heresy, and that denying its existence is itself a form of heresy (*"haeresis est maxima"*).
- Explains the mechanism of witchcraft as a tripartite combination: the malice of the devil, the permission of God, and the cooperation of the witch.
- **The Focus on Women (Question Six):** Famous for its extreme misogyny, Part I argues that women are naturally weaker in faith, intellectually childlike, emotionally unstable, and dominated by carnal lust, which makes them the primary targets of the devil. It provides a false etymology of *femina* from *fe* (faith) and *minus* (less).

### Part II: The Practices and Remedies of Witchcraft
- Details the specific "maleficia" (evil spells/actions) allegedly performed by witches.
- Discusses how witches copulate with devils (incubi and succubi), cause illnesses, raise storms, turn men into animals, steal male members, and kill infants in the womb (with a specific section targeting midwives as the worst witches).
- Offers specific remedies, blessings, and ecclesiastical procedures to counteract these spells.

### Part III: The Legal and Procedural Guide
- Provides a detailed, step-by-step procedural manual for secular and ecclesiastical judges on how to conduct a witch trial.
- Details the rules for:
  - Receiving accusations and protecting the names of witnesses.
  - Interrogating the accused and using psychological tricks.
  - Employing torture (*probatio*) to extract confessions.
  - Sentencing and delivering convicted witches to the secular authorities for execution.

## Historical Impact and Legacy
- **The Role of the Printing Press:** Published shortly after the invention of moveable type, the *Malleus* became an instant "bestseller," spreading across Europe with unprecedented speed.
- **The Papal Bull *Summis desiderantes affectibus* (1484):** Often prefixed to the book, this bull by Pope Innocent VIII lent immense, albeit indirect, ecclesiastical authority to Kramer’s mission.
- **The Shift to Gendered Persecution:** While witchcraft was a minor or gender-neutral crime in the Middle Ages, the *Malleus* was a historical milestone that gendered the crime of witchcraft, paving the way for the "witch craze" of the 16th and 17th centuries, where roughly 80% of those executed were women.
