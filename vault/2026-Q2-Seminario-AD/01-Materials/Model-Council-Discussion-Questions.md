# Discussion Questions for Model Council Analysis

## Core Debate Questions

1. **Prototype Selection**: Should the seminar focus on a contestation platform (DueProcess.AI/GovLens) or a blockchain contract system (PúblicaBlock)? What are the pedagogical and analytical advantages of each approach?

2. **Blockchain Role**: Is blockchain best used as a primary demonstrative tool (Gemini's view) or as a limited integrity-verification component (Claude/GPT's view)? How does this choice affect the seminar's message about technology neutrality?

3. **Audience Focus**: Should the seminar target administrative law practitioners facing automated decisions (Claude/GPT) or public contract lawyers and managers (Gemini)? How does audience selection shape the seminar's priorities?

## Conceptual Questions

4. **Efficiency as Political Language**: How does Claude's concept of "efficiency as a political performative category" change our understanding of technological disputes in public administration?

5. **Digital Inclusion Metric**: How does GPT's use of TIC Domicílios 2024 data (22% significant connectivity) transform abstract "digital inclusion" discussions into concrete accessibility concerns?

6. **Smart Contract Tensions**: How does Gemini's insight about smart contracts conflicting with excessive administrative contract clauses create a valuable didactic opportunity for classroom discussion?

## Application Questions

7. **Implementation Balance**: How should the consolidated GovLens/DueProcess.AI prototype balance the need for blockchain integrity verification with the LGPD's requirements for data correction, deletion, and human review?

8. **Technology Democracy**: How does the seminar's core thesis ("Administrative Public 4.0 only legitimate when efficiency subordinated to democratic contestability") apply to specific technologies like AI decision systems, smart city sensors, or blockchain-based contracts?

9. **Risk Assessment**: Which of the identified risks (algorithmic opacity, discrimination, superficial motivation, improper delegation, urban surveillance, forced self-service exclusion, blockchain immutability fetish, automated legal defense) poses the greatest threat to democratic legitimacy in Administrative Public 4.0, and why?

10. **Final Synthesis**: How does the proposed final question - "Does digital public administration decide better or just decide faster with less possibility of comprehension, contestation, and democratic participation?" - encapsulate the seminar's core concerns?
