## A.1 Corpus composition

The corpus integrates 314 instances of female allegorical figures deployed on sovereign or quasi-sovereign visual media — coins, stamps, banknotes, public sculpture, juridical-architectural ornament, and the editorial bookplate. Items were gathered through a dual-agent protocol (SCOUT — archival sweep; IconoCode — interpretive coding) documented in Chapter 2 of the thesis manuscript. The present article restricts substantive analysis to a subset of paradigmatic cases from five national trajectories (France, Germany, United Kingdom, United States, Brazil); the full corpus serves as background distribution against which those cases are situated.

Brazil is the second-largest national subset (15.0 %), a proportion that reflects the present project's primary research site (PPGD/UFSC) and the comparatively under-studied character of Brazilian sovereign iconography in the international literature.

### A.1.2 Regime distribution

Items were classified under one of four iconocratic regimes. The regime taxonomy was iteratively developed during coding: the article body theorises three (FOUNDATIONAL, NORMATIVE, MILITARY); the corpus has identified an emergent fourth, **contra-alegoria**, designating images that operate against the iconocratic logic by inverting, parodying, or de-allegorising the female sovereign body. Contra-allegorical items are reserved for separate treatment in a forthcoming article.

## A.2 Endurecimento scoring

### A.2.1 Scoring protocol and scale

Each corpus item was assigned an ordinal score (0–3) on each of the ten endurecimento indicators enumerated in §2.3 of the article body. The aggregate *endurecimento score* is the arithmetic mean of the ten indicators, rounded to two decimal places. Scoring was performed by the principal investigator informed by art-historical convention and cross-checked against the corpus.

**On the scale ceiling.** Two scoring conventions coexist in the project documentation. The article body (§2.3, current version 2026-05-08) describes a 0–4 scale where 4 designates "extreme" purification. The empirical corpus, however, has been coded on a 0–3 scale; no item exceeds 3.00. The 0–4 formulation should be read as a *theoretical* upper bound — a placeholder for cases of total purification that the corpus has not yet documented — rather than as the operational scale of the present coding. A subsequent revision will harmonise the two: either the corpus is re-coded with the 0–4 convention (assigning 4 to items currently at 3 that admit further intensification) or the article body is reset to 0–3 with the caveat that purification has no documented ceiling. The substantive argument of the article — that *dessexualization* alone, not the aggregate score, acts as the survival threshold — does not depend on which convention is adopted.

Mean = 0.70 (LOW). The corpus skews substantially toward the low end of the scale: nearly two-thirds of items score below 1.00. The focal cases analysed in the article body (Educational $5 at 1.9, Semeuse 1F at 2.5, Germania Reichspost at 2.8) are therefore *outliers in the upper tail* of the corpus distribution, not typical exemplars. This observation reinforces, rather than undermines, the threshold argument: the comparative paucity of high-endurecimento bodies in the historical record is itself evidence that the visual-political system tolerates a wide range of allegorical morphologies *up to but not beyond* the dessexualization threshold.

The corpus concentrates in the long nineteenth and early twentieth centuries (1860–1920 accounts for ~ 70 % of dated items), with a sharp peak in the 1910s (32 items, dominated by First-World-War-era postal and numismatic production). This temporal mass is consistent with the article's argument that iconocracy as a *bureaucratic-massified* phenomenon — the female allegory on coins, stamps, and banknotes handled daily by citizens — is a creature of the late-nineteenth-century administrative state.

Earlier items (Brant, Ripa, *ius commune* ornament, Reformation-era allegory) are retained for diachronic depth but do not bear directly on the present article's argument. They will be analysed in the thesis chapter on the longue durée of juridical iconography.

## A.4 Caveats and limitations

1. **Interpretive coding.** Scores are interpretive judgements informed by art-historical convention, not psychometric measurements. The ordinal scale supports comparative description and rank ordering; it does not support strong arithmetic operations on aggregate scores. The threshold argument is robust to this caveat because it depends on the **single-indicator** difference (dessexualization) between Educational and Semeuse, not on an arithmetic claim about aggregate means.

2. **Single-coder bias.** All coding to date has been performed by the principal investigator. Inter-rater reliability has not yet been established. A second-coder validation pass is planned for the thesis defence preparation phase.

3. **Coverage gaps.** The corpus over-represents France and the United Kingdom (archival accessibility) and under-represents Latin American republics other than Brazil, the Iberian Peninsula, Eastern Europe, and the non-anglophone Caribbean. The article's typology is explicitly five-country and does not claim global generality.

4. **Regime taxonomy is provisional.** The four-regime scheme (fundacional / normativo / militar / contra-alegoria) emerged inductively. The "contra-allegorical" category in particular is recent (8 items, all post-2025 ingestion) and may be revised or sub-divided as coverage grows.

5. **Date uncertainty.** Forty-nine per cent of items lack a confirmed year. Most of these are public sculptures whose dating depends on archival research not yet completed, or items whose support (e.g., series banknotes) had production windows spanning multiple years. Decadal aggregation is robust to ± 5-year uncertainty; finer temporal claims await per-item dating.

## A.5 Reproducibility

The corpus is held at `anavvanzin/Research`, under `iconocracy-corpus/corpus/corpus-data.json`. Each item has a stable identifier (`SCOUT-NNN`), an ABNT 6023:2025 citation string, source URL (where the item is publicly accessible), and the coder's *coded_at* timestamp. The dual-agent ingestion protocol (SCOUT → IconoCode) is documented in Chapter 2 of the thesis manuscript and is reproducible against any Gallica / Numista / Library-of-Congress query. Replication of the statistical tables in this appendix is available via the script `tese/artigos/scripts/appendix-a-stats.py` (to be added; query templates are in the chat log of 2026-06-05).
