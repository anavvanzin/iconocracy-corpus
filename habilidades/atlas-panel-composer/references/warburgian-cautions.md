# Methodological cautions

Before declaring a relation, classify it:

1. Documented transmission: evidence connects the objects historically.
2. Probable circulation: routes and dates make influence plausible, but evidence is incomplete.
3. Typological recurrence: a broader visual formula recurs without a proven direct line.
4. Formal analogy: visual similarity only.
5. Exploratory association: a deliberately speculative juxtaposition used to generate a question.

Do not collapse these categories.

Reject or downgrade a relation when:
- the date or source is unstable;
- the similarity depends on an aggressive crop;
- the legal or political context contradicts the proposed continuity;
- a more ordinary genre convention explains the resemblance;
- the link survives only through vague words such as power, memory, body, or violence.
