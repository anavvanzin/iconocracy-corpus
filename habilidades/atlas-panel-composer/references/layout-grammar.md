# Layout grammar

## Basic structures

### Field with anchor
One dominant object anchors the field; smaller objects complicate or contest it. Use when one image carries the institutional formula under study.

### Diptych under pressure
Two primary objects face each other, with minor objects acting as evidence, interruption, or mediation. Use for inversion, translation, or ideological conflict.

### Rhythmic sequence
A row or broken line shows mutation across time or media. Avoid turning it into a simple chronology; make each interval meaningful.

### Constellation
Several objects orbit an absent or unresolved center. Use when the missing category, suppressed body, or invisible institution is the argument.

### Fractured grid
A grid establishes comparison, then one or more breaks expose what the taxonomy cannot contain.

## Spatial variables

- Adjacency implies a strong relation; justify it.
- Distance can mark weak transmission, rupture, or unresolved comparison.
- Scale signals argumentative priority, not historical importance.
- Overlap should be rare and must signify reuse, suppression, or superimposition.
- Empty space is an interval, not leftover canvas.
- Cropping may isolate gestures or attributes, but retain a full-object reference elsewhere.
