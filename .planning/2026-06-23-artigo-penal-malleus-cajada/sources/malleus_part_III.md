## Question XIII — Before Formal Examination and Torture (Eighth Action)

### Key Excerpts for Article Section 2 (Tecnologia do Corpo Confitente)

**On confession as prerequisite for death sentence:**
> "For common justice demands that a witch should not be condemned to death unless she is convicted by her own confession."

**On three ways to establish manifest heresy (citing St. Bernard):**
The accused may be judged "manifestly taken" in heresy by any one of:
1. Evidence of the fact (direct)
2. Legitimate production of witnesses
3. The accused's own confession

Each is independently sufficient to proceed to torture.

**On the diabolical power of taciturnity:**
> "Unless God, through a holy Angel, compels the devil to withhold his help from the witch, she will be so insensible to the pains of torture that she will sooner be torn limb from limb than confess any of the truth."

**On the necessity of torture despite this:**
> "But the torture is not to be neglected for this reason..."

**On the difficulty of extracting truth:**
> "In conclusion we may say that it is as difficult, or more difficult, to compel a witch to tell the truth as it is to exorcise a person possessed of the devil."

## Question XV — Continuation of Torture and Signs of Witchcraft

### Key Excerpts for Article Section 2

**On varying interrogation methods (metáfora médica):**
> "just as the same medicine is not applicable to all the members, but there are various and distinct salves for each several member, so not all heretics or those accused of heresy are to be subjected to the same method of questioning, examination and torture…"

**On inability to weep as sign of witchcraft:**
> "If he wishes to find out whether she is endowed with a witch's power of preserving silence, let him take note whether she is able to shed tears when standing in his presence, or when being tortured."

The text claims inability to weep is a "most certain sign" of witchcraft.

**On the conjuration for tears (Latin prayer formula):**
> "I conjure you by the bitter tears shed on the Cross by our Saviour the Lord JESUS Christ for the salvation of the world, and by the burning tears poured in the evening hour over His wounds by the most glorious Virgin MARY, His Mother, and by all the tears which have been shed here in this world by the Saints and Elect of God, from whose eyes He has now wiped away all tears, that if you be innocent you do now shed tears, but if you be guilty that you shall by no means do so."

**On protective measures for judges:**
> "they must not allow themselves to be touched physically by the witch, especially in any contract of their bare arms or hands…"

**On leading the accused backward:**
> "if it can conveniently be done, the witch should be led backward into the presence of the Judge and his assessors."

**On shaving the entire body:**
> "the hair should be shaved from every part of her body."

Justification: witches allegedly hide charms in clothing, hair, or "secret parts" to preserve silence under torture.

## Analytical Notes for the Article

### How these excerpts fit Section 2 (Tecnologia do Corpo Confitente)

1. **Question XIII** establishes the procedural logic: confession is necessary for death penalty → but confession is extracted through torture → the paradox of the "insensible body" that must nevertheless be tortured.

2. **Question XV** operationalizes the gendered body as site of proof:
   - **Tears** = sign of innocence or guilt. The body that cannot weep is the guilty body. The conjuration for tears is a ritual that *produces* the truth it claims to discover.
   - **Shaving** = the body must be stripped, searched, exposed. The witch's body harbors secrets even in hair and "secret parts."
   - **Protective measures** = the witch's body is dangerous to touch. The judge must be shielded from physical contact.
   - **Leading backward** = spatial ritual that marks the accused as inverted, unnatural.

3. **The Roper connection (2013):** Roper identifies the *Malleus* as "pedagogy misógina" — a text that *teaches* inquisitors how to read the female body. Questions XIII-XV are exactly this pedagogy in procedural form: they don't describe reality; they *produce* the witch's body through ritualized reading.
